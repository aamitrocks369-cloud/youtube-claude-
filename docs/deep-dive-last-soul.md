Source: frame-by-frame review (one frame every 2 s) of the two uploads we
have locally, *Just 5 More Minutes (It's 2:14 AM)* (3:13) and *I Told Mom I
Had Friends. I Had None.* (3:37), plus the measurements in
`reference-last-soul.md`. YouTube is blocked here and vidIQ credits are at 0
until Nov 27, so no fresh channel statistics. One third-party estimate

## 2. The formula (same in both)

1. **Title = a confession or a tiny phrase** ("Just 5 More Minutes", "I Told
   Mom I Had Friends"). End card is one word or number (`1 HOUR`, `HELLO`).
2. **Act 1 (0:00–0:40):** habit plus a lie/excuse, stakes planted, a
   relatable named problem (revenge bedtime procrastination, loneliness).
3. **Act 2 (0:40–1:30):** public cost, rival or irony, low point with
   stacked inner voices, a black theme card.
4. **Mentor scene (~1:30–1:55):** one adult/stranger, three or four lines,
   one memorable instruction ("Start with hi", "Take one hour that's yours").
5. **Act 3:** the instruction as a physical object or ritual, a repeated
   shot of the earlier failure done right, warm-light payoff.
6. **Callback ending:** the opening image or time returns inverted (2:14
   asleep; she becomes the mentor). Then the moral card.

Average ~3:20, roughly one beat every 4–6 seconds, zero narration.

## 3. Production system

- **No voice actor at all.** All dialogue is on-screen text: comic font next
  to the speaker, one colour per speaker; inner thoughts in orange italics;
  doubts stack up on screen as multiple floating lines. Theme cards are serif
  italics on black. Audio is music plus room SFX only, so it is fully
  international.
- **Phone UI is half the storytelling:** lock screens, clocks, group chats,
  Mom/Dad threads, shopping order, alarm. Cheap to make (2D overlays), and
  every viewer reads it instantly.
- **Clock ladder** (`9:30 → 11:04 → 2:09 → 2:47`) shows time passing
  without narration.
- **One rig, re-dressed:** black sphere heads, white eyes with lids/brows
  (no mouth), accessory = identity (red headband, pink scrunchie, sun hat
  with glasses, cap and dog, curly hair with headphones). Crowds reuse the
  same rig in different colours.
- **Few sets, reused:** bedroom, school, gym, arena; elevator, apartment,
  hallway, stairwell, bus stop, rooftop. The stairwell shot is used twice on
  purpose.
- **Light carries mood:** blue-grey night for the problem, golden
  hour/arena lights for the payoff.
- **Camera:** slow push or drift on almost every shot, 3–4 s shots, wide →
  over-the-shoulder → close-up → POV insert; shallow depth of field.

## 4. What this means for our channel (crime / horror tragedies)

Last Soul is *uplifting* slice of life. We want *tragedy*, crime and horror.
The engine transfers almost unchanged; the third act flips:

| Last Soul | Our version |
|---|---|
| Habit + small lie | A secret, a bad choice, or one ignored warning |
| Mentor gives the fix | The warning is given and ignored, or comes too late |
| Golden payoff | The cost lands; cold light stays |
| Callback, inverted happily | Callback, inverted tragically (same shot, someone missing) |
| Moral card `1 HOUR` | One-word card that recontextualises (e.g. `READ`, `14 MISSED CALLS`) |

What to keep exactly: no narration (text plus phone UI), recurring cast
with one accessory each, a small set library reused across episodes, cold →
warm (or warm → cold) colour arc, the callback ending, 3–4 minute long-form
plus 45–60 s Shorts cut from the strongest 1–2 beats.

What to add for our goals:

- **A shared world.** Same apartment building or small town, so characters
  cross over between stories (Last Soul doesn't do this yet; it is our edge).
- **A hook in the first 2 seconds** for Shorts (an ending shown first, then
  "3 days earlier").
- **Real, named phenomena** like "revenge bedtime procrastination": scam
  tactics, bystander effect, carbon monoxide, phone tracking. Fiction built
  on a true mechanism is safe to monetise and gets shared.
- Avoid real victims and real cases.

Honest caveat: Last Soul is craft-heavy and, as far as we can tell, still a
small channel. It proves the style, not the audience size. The tragedy and
crime angle has wider demand; the style gives us the look.
