## Script anatomy (Supermarket)

- **Cold open (0:00–0:48):** a cold, true-sounding observation ("Automatic
  doors open for anything that walks up to them. They do not check for a
  pulse."), then an exact time (2:14 a.m.), an exact place (aisle seven) and
  an ordinary routine broken by one wrong detail. It ends on a hook line:
  "It is the last voice you will hear for 14 days."
- **Second person, present tense,** with short declarative sentences and a
  deadpan voice.
- **Numbered problems:** "First problem, the glass… Second problem, the
  rot… Third problem, the shutter… Fourth problem, the living." Each one gets
  a clever, concrete fix.
- **A refrain:** "New store policy: nobody gets in." It is amended as the
  story grows ("Policy one, amended. Nobody gets in, but the roof is open"),
  and the title comes back as the theme.
- **Precise numbers everywhere:** 9,000 cans at 4 a day is six years; 4
  hours for a fridge, 2 days for a freezer; 40 seconds; a ton per pallet;
  300 heads.
- **Real-world facts as survival logic:** magnetic fire doors fail without
  power; emergency lights last 90 minutes; charcoal indoors kills.
- **Store items used as tools,** named by aisle: cat litter, alarm clocks,
  ladders, camping stove.
- **Rule-based monsters:** "They don't hunt. They repeat." The dead keep
  their routines, which is the same idea as our anomaly rules.
- **Escalation by day,** with a twist every 1–2 minutes: day 3, 5, 6, 9, 10
  (the power comes back), 14 (the living knock), 19, 22, 30.
- **A recurring character loop:** Dana the manager. She leaves, her car
  arrives and she doesn't get out, the seat is empty, then she comes back
  every night at 10 to start her shift. It is used for the emotional final
  line.
- **Ending:** a day-30 inventory ("People, two. Cats, one. Cans, plenty.
  Customers, 31 a night."), the theme ("Alone, you would not have seen day
  23"), and the Dana callback.

| Numbered problems | Seven rules |

| Aisle-numbered tools | Office items as tools (badge, stapler, fire door) |
| Day counter | Shift and day counter on the HUD |

Gaps in our current scripts: no ticking timeline (days), no human through-line,
fewer precise numbers, and no clever solutions. Our rules are prohibitions,
not problem-solving.

## Visual system (per vidIQ AI watch)

- **Look:** stickmen with circle heads and dot eyes, thick slightly sketchy
  black line, identity carried by an accessory (red cap, hair bun with
  lanyard). Night palette of muted purple, grey and teal; warm sunset for the
  rooftop; blinding white for the power-on scare.
- **Pace:** a cut every 2–5 s, mostly static framing with occasional pans,
  stepped zooms, and top-down floor plans.
- **Captions:** word-by-word highlight with a neon glow.
- **Audio:** a sparse low drone with sharp, practical SFX (shutter grind,
  knocks in threes, alarm clocks). The muzak blast cuts to silence for shock.
- **Recurring templates:**
  1. Problem card: beige `#F5EBE1` full screen, icon, `PROBLEM 1: THE GLASS`
  2. Policy sticky note: `NEW STORE POLICY #1`, with strike-throughs and
     amendments
  3. Data card: icon arrays and comparisons (cans per day, fridge 4 h vs
     freezer 2 d, built for 1 man vs 300 people)
  4. Floor plan: top-down aisles with a red route and distances in yards
  5. Day card: black screen, `DAY 10`
  6. Inventory ledger: `INVENTORY · DAY 30` (people, cats, cans, customers)
